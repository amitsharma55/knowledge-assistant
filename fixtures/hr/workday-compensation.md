# Compensation

How compensation is structured in Workday, who can approve a change, and what
the review cycle looks like. Compensation attaches to a position's grade and
job family, not to the individual, so a compensation question is usually a
position question first.

## Compensation bands

Every grade and job family combination has a band with a minimum, midpoint and
maximum. Bands are reviewed annually against market data and published
internally each January.

Engineering bands for the current year:

- Engineer I, grade E1 — minimum $78,000, midpoint $92,000, maximum $106,000
- Engineer II, grade E2 — minimum $95,000, midpoint $112,000, maximum $129,000
- **Senior Engineer, grade E3 — minimum $118,000, midpoint $140,000, maximum
  $162,000**
- Staff Engineer, grade E4 — minimum $145,000, midpoint $172,000, maximum
  $199,000
- Principal Engineer, grade E5 — minimum $178,000, midpoint $210,000, maximum
  $242,000

Bands are national. A geographic differential is applied on top for a small
number of high-cost locations, and is a separate multiplier on the position
rather than a different band.

Where someone sits in a band is expected to track experience in role rather
than tenure: the midpoint represents a fully competent performer in the
position, not a long-serving one.

## Approval for a compensation change

Routine changes within band need the worker's manager and the next manager up.
The threshold that changes the process is the band maximum.

A **compensation** change **above** the band maximum requires three
**approval**s, in order:

1. The manager of the worker's supervisory organization
2. That manager's own manager
3. The HR business partner for the division

All three are required; the HR business partner cannot approve on their own,
and a manager cannot approve their own organization's exception without the
level above. Above-band requests also require a written justification, which is
retained on the position and reviewed at the next band refresh.

Above-band placements are expected to be rare and temporary. Where one persists
across two review cycles, it is treated as evidence the position is graded
wrongly rather than the person paid unusually, and the position is regraded.

## Review cycle

- Bands are refreshed each **January** against market data
- The annual compensation review runs in **March**, effective **April 1**
- Off-cycle changes are permitted for promotions, market adjustments and
  retention, and follow the same approval rules
- A promotion is a position change, so the new position's band applies
  immediately rather than at the next cycle

## What is visible to whom

- A worker sees their own compensation and the band for their position
- A manager sees compensation for everyone in their supervisory organization
  and below
- Band structures themselves are published internally and are not confidential
- Individual compensation outside a manager's own organization is restricted,
  and no downstream integration exposes it
